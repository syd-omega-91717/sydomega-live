THE SCIENCE & PHILOSOPHY OF PERSONAL PRINCIPLES
RAY DALIO — Principles (2017)
Dalio argues that principles are "fundamental truths that serve as foundations for behavior." He distinguishes between life principles (how you live) and work principles (how you operate). Writing and stress-testing your principles produces consistent, values-aligned decision-making — especially under pressure, where emotions corrupt judgment. Dalio famously coded 200+ principles into Bridgewater's "idea meritocracy."
MARCUS AURELIUS — Meditations (~170 CE)
The Meditations are a private journal of operating principles — written for the author, not for publication. Aurelius repeatedly returns to core laws: distinguish what is in your control from what isn't (Stoic dichotomy); act for the common good; meet every event with equanimity. Revisiting and rewriting principles reinforces them as automatic behavioral guides under pressure.
CHARLIE MUNGER — Poor Charlie's Almanack (2005)
Munger's mental models framework is an applied-principles system: build a latticework of models from multiple disciplines (psychology, physics, biology, economics) and apply them across decision contexts. "Elementary worldly wisdom is knowing your principles well enough that they apply themselves." A sovereign principle library functions as exactly this — a personal latticework of verified truths.
JAMES CLEAR — Atomic Habits (2018) on Identity-Based Change
Clear demonstrates that lasting behavioral change must be rooted in identity: "I am the type of person who..." rather than "I want to..." Written principles operationalize identity — they make abstract self-concepts concrete and actionable. When a decision conflicts with a principle, identity pressure resolves it without willpower depletion (Muraven & Baumeister, 2000).
VIKTOR FRANKL — Man's Search for Meaning (1946)
Frankl's logotherapy shows that meaning-driven behavior is more resilient than pleasure-seeking or pain-avoiding behavior. Principles that are grounded in a clear purpose (your sovereign mission) function as motivational anchors that survive adversity. "He who has a why can bear almost any how." — Nietzsche, as cited by Frankl.
NASSIM TALEB — Antifragile (2012)
Taleb argues that robust principles must be tested against adversity — principles derived only from comfortable conditions fail under stress. Skin-in-the-game (2018) adds: principles must have personal stakes. A sovereign principle you've never been tested against is theory, not law. Record violations and how you responded — that is your real operating system.
