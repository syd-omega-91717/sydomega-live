I
MORNING MEDITATION (PREMEDITATIO MALORUM)
Spend 5–10 minutes anticipating what could go wrong today. Not to worry, but to prepare. Pre-visualizing adversity removes its sting. The Stoic is never surprised — they've already faced it in imagination.
II
EVENING EXAMINATION (SENECA'S AUDIT)
Before sleep, review the entire day: "What weakness did I overcome? What did I resist? In what way did I improve?" Three questions: Where did I go wrong? What did I accomplish? What could I improve?
III
NEGATIVE VISUALIZATION (AMOR FATI)
Periodically imagine losing what you value most — health, relationships, freedom, possessions. Not catastrophizing, but cultivating gratitude. When you feel the loss, you appreciate the presence fully.
IV
VOLUNTARY DISCOMFORT (ASKĒSIS)
Intentionally practice discomfort. Cold showers, fasting, simple meals. Seneca advised living for a few days each month as if in poverty. Purpose: prove to yourself you can endure, and that feared losses aren't essential.
V
VIEW FROM ABOVE (MARCUS AURELIUS)
Zoom out mentally: room → city → continent → planet → solar system. "How small a fragment of the boundless infinity of time each man lives." This perspective dissolves petty concerns and reconnects you to what matters.
VI
ACTING THE ROLE (KATHÊKON)
Identify your current role — sovereign, parent, professional, friend — and ask: "What does virtue require of me in this role right now?" Happiness comes from fulfilling your proper function with complete integrity.

STOIC PHILOSOPHERS
MARCUS AURELIUS
121 – 180 CE · Roman Emperor · Philosopher
The philosopher-king. Practiced Stoicism under conditions of absolute power, pandemic, war, personal tragedy, and betrayal. Meditations written only for himself — never intended for publication.
"You have power over your mind — not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength."
"The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way."
EPICTETUS
50 – 135 CE · Former Slave · Philosopher
Born into slavery, Epictetus developed the most rigorous Stoic discipline — because freedom, for him, was never external, only internal. What happens to you is irrelevant; only your response determines your character.
"We cannot choose our external circumstances, but we can always choose how we respond to them."
"No man is free who is not master of himself."
SENECA
4 BCE – 65 CE · Statesman · Author
The most prolific Stoic writer. On the Shortness of Life, On Anger, Letters to Lucilius — some of the most readable philosophy ever written. Wealth and power are not evil, only the attachment to them.
"The whole future lies in uncertainty: live immediately."
"Begin at once to live, and count each separate day as a separate life."
ZENO OF CITIUM
334 – 262 BCE · Founder of Stoicism
After being shipwrecked and losing his fortune, Zeno founded his school — teaching in the Stoa Poikilē (painted porch), giving Stoicism its name. Logic, physics, and ethics unified around living according to nature.
"Man conquers the world by conquering himself."
"The goal of life is to live in agreement with Nature."

BUILT ON STOICISM
STOICISM & MODERN NEUROSCIENCE
The Stoics discovered through philosophy what neuroscience confirmed: the amygdala fires before the prefrontal cortex. The 90-second rule (Jill Bolte Taylor) — any emotion lasts 90 seconds neurologically. Beyond that, you are choosing to stay in it. The Stoic pause is neurological recalibration.
COGNITIVE BEHAVIOURAL THERAPY
CBT, the world's most evidence-backed psychotherapy, is Stoicism formalized. Aaron Beck and Albert Ellis directly cited Epictetus: "Men are disturbed not by events, but by their opinions about events." Reframing, thought records, and behavioural activation all derive from Stoic practice.
NEGATIVE VISUALIZATION
Modern research on hedonic adaptation confirms the Stoic technique of premeditatio malorum. Visualizing loss increases gratitude (positive psychology) and reduces anxiety through exposure desensitization. Athletes use this as "stress inoculation training" — pre-experiencing adversity to remove its sting.
THE SOVEREIGN PHILOSOPHY
Stoicism is not emotional suppression — it is emotional sovereignty. The Stoic feels fully, then chooses their response. Viktor Frankl in the concentration camps discovered what Marcus Aurelius built an empire on: between stimulus and response lies freedom. That gap is where sovereignty lives.
