TECHNOLOGY RADAR ORIGIN
ThoughtWorks Technology Radar (2010) popularized quadrant-ring categorization of technology adoption. The four rings — Adopt, Trial, Assess, Hold — represent opinionated guidance based on production experience. The key insight: not using a technology is also a choice that needs reasoning.
ThoughtWorks (2010). Technology Radar Vol. 1
CHASM CROSSING
Moore (1991): technology adoption follows a bell curve with a chasm between Early Adopters and Early Majority. Positioning yourself in the Early Adopter segment of technologies that cross the chasm provides compounding advantage — you have years of depth before the mainstream catches up.
Moore, G.A. (1991). Crossing the Chasm
SCIENTIFIC METHOD
Experiments require falsifiable hypotheses (Popper, 1934). A good experiment hypothesis: (1) specific and measurable, (2) testable within a defined timeframe, (3) connected to a clear mechanism. Most innovation failures are due to untested assumptions, not execution problems.
Popper, K. (1934). The Logic of Scientific Discovery
GARTNER HYPE CYCLE
Gartner (1995): Innovation Trigger → Peak of Inflated Expectations → Trough of Disillusionment → Slope of Enlightenment → Plateau of Productivity. The best investments are in the Trough — when a technology is real but pessimism suppresses adoption and prices. Most people buy at the Peak.
Gartner Inc. (1995). Hype Cycle Framework
FAST FAILURE PRINCIPLE
Thomke (2003): the cost of knowledge is the cost of experiments. Digital technology enables cheap, fast experiments. The goal is not to avoid failure but to fail fast, cheaply, and informatively. Each experiment should answer exactly one question, with a clear binary verdict: confirmed or refuted.
Thomke, S.H. (2003). Experimentation Matters, HBS Press
COMBINATORIAL INNOVATION
Weitzman (1998): growth comes from recombining existing ideas in new ways. The innovation frontier expands when you have breadth across domains (tools, frameworks, techniques) AND depth in at least one. The radar forces systematic breadth; experiments build depth.
Weitzman, M.L. (1998). Quarterly Journal of Economics
