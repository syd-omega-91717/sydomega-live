GOVERNANCE SCIENCE
01
RISK MATRIX
ISO 31000 risk management framework: Risk = Severity × Likelihood. A systematic risk register externalizes threat awareness, reducing cognitive load and preventing availability bias in risk perception.
02
PRECOMMITMENT DEVICES
Ulysses contracts (Ariely, Schelling): binding yourself in advance to a policy removes the temptation at the moment of weakness. Policies convert willpower problems into identity problems — far more durable.
03
DECISION JOURNALING
Annie Duke's "Thinking in Bets": track decisions before knowing outcomes. This separates decision quality from outcome quality — the only way to improve decision-making without confounding luck.
04
SECOND-ORDER THINKING
Howard Marks: ask not just "what happens next?" but "what happens after that?" Governance requires modeling second and third-order consequences, especially in high-stakes, complex domains.
05
ETHICS AS STRATEGY
Long-run game theory: cooperators outperform defectors when games repeat. Sovereign ethics are strategic as much as moral — reputational capital, trust, and relationship equity compound enormously over time.
06
CORPORATE GOVERNANCE RESEARCH
Gompers, Ishii, Metrick (2003): firms with stronger governance frameworks outperform peers by 8.5% annually. The same principle applies to personal governance: structure creates alpha.
