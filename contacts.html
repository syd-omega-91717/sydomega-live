RELATIONSHIP INTELLIGENCE — SCIENCE
DUNBAR'S NUMBER — Robin Dunbar (1992) Journal of Human Evolution
Dunbar's research on primate neocortex size and social group size established cognitive limits on stable social relationships: ~5 (intimate), ~15 (close friends), ~50 (tribe), ~150 (stable network), ~500 (acquaintances), ~1500 (recognizable faces). These limits are neurological, not cultural. Managing your network intentionally within these tiers — giving most energy to your inner 15 — produces stronger relationship quality than diffuse investment across hundreds.
WEAK TIES & OPPORTUNITY — Mark Granovetter (1973) American Journal of Sociology
Granovetter's landmark "Strength of Weak Ties" study found that job opportunities and novel information flow through weak ties (acquaintances) more than strong ties (close friends), who share your existing knowledge graph. A balanced sovereign network maintains depth in the inner circles AND breadth in the outer rings — the outer rings provide serendipitous exposure to new domains, markets, and people.
RECIPROCITY PRINCIPLE — Robert Cialdini (1984) Influence
Reciprocity is among the most powerful and universal social norms across cultures. Giving first — without transactional intent — creates asymmetric goodwill that compounds over time. Adam Grant (Give and Take, 2013) found that "Givers" reach the top of success distributions across professions: they build larger, more diverse networks because people want to help those who helped them. Sovereign relationship management means giving first and tracking, not reciprocity-accounting.
RELATIONSHIP MAINTENANCE — Duck (1994) Meaningful Relationships
Duck's relational maintenance research established that relationships decay without regular contact — not through conflict, but simply through neglect. The "proximity" and "frequency" factors of social bonding (Festinger et al. 1950) show that relationships that aren't actively maintained regress from close→tribe→network tier automatically. Proactive touch scheduling reverses this entropy.
