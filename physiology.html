HRV ZONES — RECOVERY INTERPRETATION
POOR (<20ms): Overreached, stressed, under-recovered. Reduce training intensity. Prioritize sleep and nutrition.
LOW (20-40ms): Below baseline. Monitor carefully. Reduce volume but maintain movement.
MODERATE (40-65ms): Average recovery. Normal training can proceed. Focus on sleep quality.
HIGH (65-100ms): Well recovered. Push training. Optimal state for deliberate practice and peak effort.
ELITE (>100ms): Exceptional autonomic tone. Seen in endurance athletes. Maximize training load.
THE SCIENCE OF PHYSIOLOGICAL TRACKING
HEART RATE VARIABILITY — Task Force (1996) European Heart Journal
The 1996 Task Force of the European Society of Cardiology and the North American Society standardized HRV measurement. HRV (RMSSD) reflects autonomic nervous system balance: high HRV = strong parasympathetic tone = recovery capacity. Daily HRV tracking (particularly upon waking) is more predictive of readiness and performance than training load alone. Buchheit (2014): HRV-guided training produces superior gains to fixed periodization.
VO2 MAX AS LONGEVITY PREDICTOR — Attia / Mandsager et al. (2018) JAMA Network Open
Mandsager's study of 122,007 patients found that cardiorespiratory fitness (VO2 max) was the strongest predictor of all-cause mortality — stronger than hypertension, diabetes, or smoking. Moving from "low" to "below average" fitness reduced mortality risk by 50%. The highest fitness category (elite) showed 80% reduction vs. sedentary. Peter Attia: VO2 max is "the most powerful metric in medicine."
RESTING HEART RATE & CARDIOVASCULAR HEALTH — Fox et al. (2007) Heart Journal
Fox's analysis of 129,135 patients established that each 10 BPM increase in resting heart rate correlates with a 9% increase in cardiovascular mortality. Endurance training reliably reduces resting HR by 5-25 BPM, reflecting improved cardiac efficiency and parasympathetic tone. A resting HR below 60 BPM is associated with significantly reduced all-cause mortality.
BLOOD PRESSURE TARGETS — ACC/AHA Guidelines (2017)
The 2017 ACC/AHA guidelines redefined hypertension from 140/90 to 130/80 mmHg, adding ~30 million Americans to the hypertension category. SPRINT trial (2015, NEJM): treating to a target of <120 mmHg systolic reduced cardiovascular events by 25% and all-cause mortality by 27%. Exercise (aerobic + resistance), dietary sodium reduction, and weight management are first-line interventions before pharmacotherapy.
BIOMETRIC TRACKING & BEHAVIOR CHANGE — Hernandez et al. (2011) MIT Media Lab
Consistent physiological self-monitoring produces measurable improvements in health behaviors — independent of external intervention. The "quantified self" effect (Wolf, 2009): visibility of data creates accountability loops. Daily tracking of RHR and HRV correlates with improved sleep, exercise adherence, and stress management — because measurement itself creates behavioral friction against health-undermining choices.
