SOVEREIGN IDENTITY
Erikson (1950) identity theory: stable identity is built through progressive integration of past experiences, present roles, and future commitments. A documented sovereign identity — codified mission, values, provenance — creates the psychological foundation for self-directed life construction.
Erikson, E.H. (1950). Childhood and Society
NARRATIVE IDENTITY
McAdams (1993): people construct identity as personal narrative — a coherent story that integrates past, present, and future into a meaningful whole. High-functioning individuals have detailed, coherent self-narratives. Writing your passport forces narrative coherence and self-authorship.
McAdams, D.P. (1993). The Stories We Live By
AUTHORITY AND IDENTITY
Weber (1922) identified three foundations of authority: traditional, charismatic, and rational-legal. Sovereign personal authority synthesizes all three: a rational system (this OS), charismatic vision (mission), and earned legitimacy (provenance and track record). The passport documents this authority.
Weber, M. (1922). Economy and Society
SELF-CONCEPT CLARITY
Campbell et al. (1996): self-concept clarity — how confidently and consistently you define yourself — predicts psychological well-being, resilience, and identity-consistent behavior. High SCC individuals make faster, more consistent decisions and are less susceptible to social comparison pressure.
Campbell et al. (1996). Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
