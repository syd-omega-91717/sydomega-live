THE SCIENCE OF VOCABULARY ACQUISITION

Language is the operating system of thought. Expanding your lexicon expands the resolution of your mind — enabling you to perceive, articulate, and act on distinctions invisible to those with smaller vocabularies.

SPACED REPETITION SYSTEM — Ebbinghaus (1885) & Leitner (1970)
Ebbinghaus's Forgetting Curve: memory retention decays exponentially without review. Spaced repetition exploits the Spacing Effect — reviewing material at increasing intervals (1→4→8→14→30 days) achieves 2-3× better retention with the same study time. The SM-2 algorithm (Supermemo, 1987) formalizes optimal review intervals based on recall quality. This system uses SM-2 intervals.
VOCABULARY SIZE & COMPREHENSION — Nation (2001) & Coxhead (2000)
Nation's research establishes that a 2,000-word vocabulary enables ~90% coverage of everyday speech. The Academic Word List (AWL) by Coxhead identifies 570 high-value academic words that appear across disciplines. Mastering the top 5,000-10,000 words yields near-native comprehension. Sovereign vocabulary focuses on precision, rarity, and domain depth over breadth.
CONTEXTUAL LEARNING — Krashen (1985) & Nation (2001)
The comprehensible input hypothesis (Krashen 1985) shows words are best learned in meaningful context, not isolation. Reading words in example sentences activates deeper encoding than definition memorization alone. Deliberate vocabulary study (Nation 2001) — engaging with form, meaning, and use simultaneously — produces faster, more durable learning.
RETRIEVAL PRACTICE — Roediger & Karpicke (2006)
The Testing Effect: actively recalling information (not just re-reading) strengthens memory traces significantly more than passive review. A single retrieval attempt after initial study improves long-term retention by 40-60% vs re-study. Flashcard drilling in this system leverages retrieval practice as the primary encoding mechanism.
LEXICAL SOPHISTICATION & INTELLIGENCE — Vocabulary.com / Sadoski & Paivio (2001)
Dual Coding Theory (Paivio 1971, updated 2001): encoding words alongside imagery and context creates stronger, more accessible memory traces. Etymology (word roots) enables vocabulary generalization — knowing the Latin radix allows inference of radiate, radiation, radical, eradicate. Semantic mapping clusters related words for more efficient long-term storage.
SOVEREIGN APPLICATION — Adams (2013) Skill Stacking
Scott Adams argues that being in the top 25% of two or more skills multiplies your value and uniqueness. Language precision is a multiplier across all domains — the leader who speaks with precision, the engineer who writes clearly, the sovereign who reasons in exact terms. Vocabulary is not decoration; it is operational leverage.
