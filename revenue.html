MULTIPLE INCOME STREAMS
IRS data (2019): the average millionaire has 7 income streams. These span: earned income, profit income, interest income, dividend income, rental income, capital gains, and royalty income. Each stream diversifies income risk — losing one stream doesn't eliminate income, just reduces it.
IRS Statistics of Income Division (2019)
PASSIVE INCOME LEVERAGE
Kiyosaki (1997, Rich Dad Poor Dad): the rich buy assets that generate passive income; the poor trade time for money. Each passive dollar is worth more than an active dollar because it doesn't consume hours. The ratio of passive to active income is a proxy for financial sovereignty.
Kiyosaki, R. (1997). Rich Dad Poor Dad
RECURRING REVENUE VALUATION
SaaS and subscription businesses are valued at 5–15× ARR vs. 1–3× revenue for one-time businesses. Predictable, recurring revenue earns a valuation premium because it reduces investor risk. Building subscription income streams vs. project-based income fundamentally changes your asset's terminal value.
Bessemer Venture Partners (2020). State of the Cloud Report
INCOME DIVERSIFICATION THEORY
Markowitz's diversification principle (1952) applies to income just as to investments. Uncorrelated income streams — a tech salary (correlated with tech sector), dividends (market), rental income (real estate cycle), consulting (personal reputation) — reduce the variance of total income under economic shocks.
Markowitz, H. (1952). Journal of Finance
INCOME CEILING VS. INCOME LEVERAGE
Taussig & Baker (2018): income ceilings exist for all active-income roles — you can only work so many hours. Income leverage breaks this ceiling by decoupling income from time. Digital products, IP, equity, and capital are all forms of income leverage — they scale without proportional time input.
Taussig & Baker (2018). Harvard Business Review
THE SOVEREIGNTY THRESHOLD
The financial independence number (Bengen 1994, Trinity Study 1998): passive income exceeding expenses at a 4% safe withdrawal rate creates financial sovereignty — capital works harder than you do. Every passive income stream moves you closer. Track the ratio: passive income / total expenses.
Bengen (1994) · Cooley et al. (1998)
