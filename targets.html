WHAT IS AN OBJECTIVE?
A qualitative, inspiring statement of what you want to become or achieve.

✓ Ambitious but achievable
✓ Aligns with your sovereign vision
✓ Time-boxed to the quarter
✗ Not a task list item
✗ Not a binary yes/no

Example: "Achieve sovereign financial independence by building a recurring income engine."
WHAT IS A KEY RESULT?
A measurable outcome that proves whether the Objective was achieved.

✓ Specific and numeric
✓ Outcome-focused (not activity)
✓ Challenging (60-70% confidence)
✗ Not "do X" — should be "X measured by Y"

Example: "Grow monthly recurring revenue from $0 to $5,000."
THE INTEL THEORY OF OKRs
Andy Grove created OKRs at Intel. John Doerr brought them to Google.

The key insight: Ideas are easy. Execution is everything.

OKRs close the gap between ambition and action by forcing you to define what "done" looks like — before you begin.

GOAL-SETTING THEORY
Locke & Latham's 35-year research program: specific, challenging goals lead to higher performance than vague or easy goals in 90% of studies.

OKRs operationalize this: Objectives (challenging) + Key Results (specific/measurable) = maximum execution probability.
THE QUARTERLY CYCLE
90 days is the optimal human planning horizon: long enough to matter, short enough to maintain urgency. Annual goals fail because the deadline is too distant to compel daily action.

The sovereign compounds quarterly. 4 cycles × compounding = exponential transformation in 1 year.
