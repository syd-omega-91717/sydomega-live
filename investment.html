MODERN PORTFOLIO THEORY
Markowitz (1952): diversification reduces risk without proportionally reducing return. The efficient frontier shows the set of portfolios with maximum return for a given risk level. Correlation below 1.0 between assets generates diversification benefit — hold uncorrelated assets.
Markowitz, H. (1952). Journal of Finance
FACTOR INVESTING
Fama & French (1992) three-factor model: market risk, size (small caps outperform), and value (cheap stocks outperform) explain most equity returns. Carhart (1997) added momentum as a fourth factor. Understanding factors lets you target return premia systematically rather than picking stocks.
Fama & French (1992). Journal of Finance
DOLLAR COST AVERAGING
DCA (Tobin, 1958): investing fixed amounts at regular intervals reduces timing risk by automatically buying more shares when prices are low and fewer when high. For long-horizon investors, DCA produces equal or better returns than lump sum in volatile markets with significantly lower psychological stress.
Tobin, J. (1958). Review of Economic Studies
BEHAVIORAL BIASES
Kahneman & Tversky (1979): loss aversion — losses feel ~2.5x more painful than equivalent gains feel good. This causes premature selling of winners (disposition effect) and holding losers too long. Tracking your portfolio systematically counters emotional decision-making with data.
Kahneman & Tversky (1979). Econometrica
COMPOUND RETURNS
Warren Buffett's mentor Graham (1949) called compounding the core engine of wealth. At 10% annual return: $10,000 grows to $67,275 in 20 years, $174,494 in 30 years, $452,593 in 40 years. The Rule of 72: divide 72 by the return rate to get years to double. Time is the primary variable.
Graham, B. (1949). The Intelligent Investor
REBALANCING
Bernstein (1996): systematic rebalancing to target weights forces buy-low/sell-high behavior mechanically. Annual or threshold-based rebalancing (drift >5%) adds 0.4–0.8% annual return vs. unmanaged portfolios through variance drain reduction and forced discipline.
Bernstein, W. (1996). Efficient Frontier
