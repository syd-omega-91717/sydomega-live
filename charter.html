PERSONAL CONSTITUTION
Covey (1989) recommends a personal mission statement as the cornerstone of effective living. A personal constitution goes further: it codifies values, commitments, and operating principles into a binding governance document for the self — reviewed quarterly to maintain alignment.
Covey, S.R. (1989). The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People
ULYSSES CONTRACTS
Elster (1979): a Ulysses contract is a pre-commitment device — you bind your future self to a course of action when your future state may be compromised by weakness or altered preferences. Written charters function as Ulysses contracts at the identity level, surviving momentary lapses.
Elster, J. (1979). Ulysses and the Sirens
VALUES CLARIFICATION
Schwartz (1992) identified 10 universal values organized around self-enhancement vs. self-transcendence. Explicit values clarification activates identity-protective cognition and reduces cognitive dissonance under pressure. You make better decisions when your values are externalized and visible.
Schwartz, S.H. (1992). Advances in Experimental Social Psychology
SELF-AFFIRMATION THEORY
Steele (1988): reflecting on core values buffers the self-system against threat and improves decision quality under stress. Re-reading your charter is a formal self-affirmation ritual — it re-activates your value hierarchy and reduces ego depletion before high-stakes decisions.
Steele, C.M. (1988). Advances in Experimental Social Psychology
CONSTITUTIONAL GOVERNANCE
Madison (1787, Federalist 51): constitutions work because they are harder to change than ordinary decisions. The amendment process creates friction that prevents temporary preferences from overriding fundamental commitments. Personal constitutions gain their power from the same mechanism.
Madison, J. (1787). Federalist No. 51
COMMITMENT AND CONSISTENCY
Cialdini (1984): once committed in writing, people are strongly motivated to behave consistently with that commitment. Written charters are 3-4x more powerful than private intentions because they engage both internal consistency pressure and social accountability when shared.
Cialdini, R. (1984). Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion
