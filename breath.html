SOVEREIGN BREATHING PROTOCOLS
BOX BREATHING · 4-4-4-4
Inhale 4 · Hold 4 · Exhale 4 · Hold 4. Equal ratio. Balance and composure. US Navy SEAL standard for stress control under fire.
4-7-8 CALM
Inhale 4 · Hold 7 · Exhale 8. Extended hold maximizes O₂ absorption. Long exhale triggers diving reflex. Fast anxiety reduction protocol.
COHERENCE · 5-0-5-0
Inhale 5 · Exhale 5. Six breaths per minute. Synchronizes heart rate, blood pressure, and respiratory rhythms. HRV optimization protocol.
PHYSIOLOGICAL SIGH
Double inhale through nose + long exhale. Fastest single-breath stress intervention. Re-inflates collapsed alveoli. Rapidly expels CO₂.
POWER BREATH · 6-0-2-0
Long inhale 6 · Short exhale 2. Sympathetic activation. Energy and focus amplification. Pre-performance arousal protocol.
RESONANT · 5.5-5.5
5.5-second inhale, 5.5-second exhale: about 5.5 breaths a minute, the resonance frequency of the heart-lung system. Maximizes HRV and synchronizes the nervous system.
TRIANGLE · 4-4-8
Inhale 4 · Hold 4 · Exhale 8. A long exhale after a short hold. Deep calm before peak output.
ENERGIZE · 2-0-2-0
Rapid, even breaths. Quick activation when you need to wake the system up. Stop if you feel lightheaded.
WIM HOF METHOD · guide only
Thirty rapid breaths, then retain, then recover; repeat three or four rounds. Alkalizes the blood and raises adrenaline. The timer does not run this pattern. Never practise it while driving or in water.
UJJAYI (OCEAN BREATH) · guide only
Constrict the back of the throat on both inhale and exhale to make an ocean-like sound. Builds internal heat and focus; used in yoga. The timer does not run this pattern.
WAKING BREATH · 1-0-1-0
Natural breath with awareness. Baseline. First practice before adding ratios. Grounds the nervous system in present-moment sensation.

VAGAL TONE & HEART RATE VARIABILITY
Porges, S.W. (1995) — Orienting in a defensive world: Mammalian modifications of our evolutionary heritage. Psychophysiology, 32(4)
Stephen Porges' Polyvagal Theory explains how slow, diaphragmatic breathing directly activates the vagus nerve — the primary pathway of the parasympathetic nervous system. Extended exhalations increase heart rate variability (HRV), a key biomarker of nervous system resilience. Controlled breathwork is one of the only direct access points to the autonomic nervous system available to conscious volition.
BOX BREATHING (TACTICAL BREATHING)
Sovik, R. (2000) — The science of breathing. Progress in Brain Research, 122 / US Navy SEAL Training documentation
Box breathing (4-4-4-4: inhale, hold, exhale, hold) is used in US Navy SEAL training and elite athletic performance programs. The equal-ratio pattern activates both sympathetic and parasympathetic branches simultaneously, producing a coherent, balanced state — alert without anxiety. Research confirms significant reductions in cortisol, improvements in attentional control, and enhanced emotional regulation after 5-10 minute sessions.
4-7-8 BREATHING & ANXIETY REDUCTION
Weil, A. (2015) — Breathing: The Master Key to Self-Healing (audio program) / Brown, R.P. & Gerbarg, P.L. (2005) — Sudarshan Kriya yogic breathing. Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine
The 4-7-8 pattern (inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8) is derived from pranayama practices and studied in clinical anxiety contexts. The 7-count hold at full inhalation maximizes oxygen absorption, while the extended 8-count exhale triggers the diving reflex — a hard-wired parasympathetic response that reduces heart rate and calms the amygdala. Studies show measurable blood pressure reduction after single sessions.
PHYSIOLOGICAL SIGH & CO₂ REGULATION
Balban, M.Y. et al. (2023) — Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal. Cell Reports Medicine, 4(1)
Stanford research led by Andrew Huberman and Mark Krasnow identified the "physiological sigh" — a double inhale through the nose followed by a long exhale — as the fastest single-breath intervention for reducing acute stress. It works by re-inflating collapsed alveoli and rapidly expelling accumulated CO₂. A 2023 RCT confirmed cyclic sighing improved mood and reduced physiological arousal more effectively than mindfulness meditation over 5 minutes.
RESONANT BREATHING & COHERENCE
McCraty, R. (2016) — Heart-Brain Coherence. HeartMath Institute Research Center / Lehrer, P.M. et al. (2000) — Heart rate variability biofeedback. Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback
Breathing at approximately 5.5-6 breaths per minute (roughly 5 seconds in, 5 seconds out) produces cardiac coherence — a state where heart rate, blood pressure, and respiratory rhythms synchronize. HeartMath Institute research shows coherent states improve cognitive performance, emotional regulation, and immune function. This is the physiological mechanism behind the sovereign practice of slow, rhythmic conscious breathing.
WHY BREATH CONTROLS STATE
The vagus nerve connects the brain to the body, and breathing is the only autonomic function you can consciously control. By controlling the breath you modulate the autonomic nervous system directly, shifting between sympathetic (fight-or-flight) and parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) states.
CO₂ TOLERANCE
Most people breathe too fast. The urge to breathe is triggered by CO₂, not by a lack of oxygen. Building CO₂ tolerance through breath training extends calm performance under pressure.
