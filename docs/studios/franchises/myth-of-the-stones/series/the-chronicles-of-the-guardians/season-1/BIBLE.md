# THE CHRONICLES OF THE GUARDIANS — Season 1: The Forgotten Order (Bible, Draft 1, PROPOSED)

**Status: SEASON BIBLE DRAFT 1.** Not approved, not produced. This is a bible and eight beat sheets. **Episodes 101 to 108 now exist as short
first drafts** (a cold open and three condensed acts, about 14-16 of about 50 pages each; see "Scripting changes" below). The brief
requires each episode to get a full-length screenplay, and those are the next wave. Written under the founder's delegation on 2026-11-07,
after the founder approved the story of *The Seven Stones* (see `config/omega-studio-productions.json`). Format: 8 episodes of about
50 minutes. Registry ids: `myth-of-the-stones`, series title `The Chronicles of the Guardians`.

## The structural decision (read this first)

The founder's brief lists Season 1 episodes (First Signal, Hidden Bloodline, Keeper, Broken Kingdom, Traitor's Memory, Fifth Stone,
Sundering, Last Guardian) that are almost the same beats as the film. A series that retells the film is wasted, and one that follows
it cannot contain them. **Decision: Season 1 is the year before the film.** It keeps every brief title and every beat's *intent*, but runs
in the eleven months leading up to the film's opening day, and **episode 8 ends on the film's first present-day scene**. Season 2 (the
brief's "consequences of the first film") then follows the film. The brief's final beat for episode 8, "the discovery of the twelve houses",
is kept as a *discovery of the existence of the houses*; the film pays it off when the twelve signs light.

**Release order is free.** The season is written so it can be watched before or after the film without spoiling the film's twists
(see Continuity locks). It should *not* reveal that Seraphine is Eryndor to a viewer who has not seen the film unless the founder wants
that; the bible's default is that episode 3 shows it to the audience while Auren and Lyra remain ignorant, which the film then pays off dramatically.

## Premise and tone

Eleven months before a buried temple wakes, five people are being drawn toward it without knowing each other: a restorer who hears a note
under the world, an archaeologist whose funding has just collapsed, a courteous security consultant with a second loyalty, a bookseller
who has been waiting four thousand years, and the kindest antagonist in fantasy, who is searching for his daughter's world. Tone: grounded
mystery that slowly admits the impossible; long conversations, quiet dread, one great set piece per episode. The season's question:
**who built the Loom, and why do the people who say they saved the world keep lying about it?**

## Recurring motifs
- **9.1717 Hz.** Rises in each episode's cold open and is heard by a different person each time. Season-wide score spine.
- **The paper bird.** First seen in the film's prologue. In Season 1 it appears folded by Malachar in episode 1, by Liora in 7, and left on a table in 8.
- **"Hold the other end."** Ithren's phrase; also what Solenne says in Zodiac Wars (registry C-18). Spoken once per season, in episode 7.
- **Two-key locks.** Every set piece contains one door that needs two hands.
- **Opening sequence:** nine concentric rings draw themselves, seven inscribed and two left empty. **Closing sequence:** the rings fade, and a single low tone holds under the credits.

## Continuity locks with the film (a season that breaks one of these is wrong)
1. **Malachar holds exactly two stones at the film's opening (Time and Energy).** Season 1 gives him Energy in episode 6; Time he has had for 300 years.
2. **Seraphine and Malachar do not meet face to face** in Season 1. The film's Act III reunion, after "three hundred and eleven years", must stay a reunion.
3. **Auren and Lyra do not meet.** They are within a city of each other in episode 8 and the film begins before they do.
4. **Auren has never seen the Citadel**, the Crystal Depths or Karth Vael until the film. He has only heard of Kharesh.
5. **Auren does not stop time** until the film's pit scene. Season 1 shows only visions and one involuntary stutter in the lab.
6. **Torin has not been seen by Auren** and is "recently hired by funders" from Lyra's view. He joins Lyra's project in episode 6.
7. **The Citadel's Lamp has not spoken to anyone but Seraphine** in ninety years (film: "ninety years").
8. **Torin's age and history:** orphan, visited by Malachar from about twelve and raised by him from fifteen, placed with the Wardens at eighteen, expelled at twenty-two, which is the "fell out twenty years ago" of the film (he is 42 in the film). *Corrected while scripting 105: the earlier text said he joined at twenty-two, which cannot hold with the film.*
9. **Frequency is 9.1717 Hz** everywhere (registry C-07).

## Episode guide

### 1. THE FIRST SIGNAL
*Cold open:* Ithren, four thousand years ago, buries a small vessel in the Citadel floor and says, to nobody, "For whoever holds the other end."
*Logline:* Auren hears the note under the world. Lyra loses her funding. Somewhere a man folds a paper bird.
- A: Auren's coins hum in the lab; first stutter of time lasts a blink; Nell sees it. He tells no one.
- B: Lyra's grant is cancelled; a mysterious donor offers a "security-backed" renewal (Torin's first appearance, offscreen).
- C: Malachar at the abbey sanctum folding a paper bird; two niches filled, five empty; Vesper reports a lead.
- Flashback: Aurelith festival morning, wordless, Liora on the road.
*Cliffhanger:* Auren wakes standing at his window with "91717" written across the glass in condensation.

### 2. THE HIDDEN BLOODLINE
*Logline:* Auren opens his mother's tin box. The night she vanished is shown.
- A: the tin box, the notebook page with nine rings (as in the film); Auren decodes "Kharesh" but misreads it as a surname.
- B (flashback): Isolde Vale, fifteen years ago, a keeper of the Guardians' line, leaves her nine-year-old son with a neighbour and goes to the bookshop under the railway arch. Seraphine sees the child standing on a chair reaching for a high book (the film's anecdote). Isolde hides a vessel and leaves; the Reunion arrive too late.
- C: Hollis and Tuan find the lattice diagram on a slab at Anvar Ridge; Lyra recognises the structure.
*Cliffhanger:* Auren sees a podcast clip of Lyra describing "a network on stone", and his hands start to tremble.

### 3. THE KEEPER
*Logline:* A bookshop owner who has kept a secret for four thousand years considers breaking it. **This is the episode where the audience learns Seraphine is Eryndor.** Auren and Lyra do not.
- A: Seraphine alone in the Citadel with the Lamp; ninety years of silence; she reads the records of the seven; she decides not to approach Auren yet because "every time we reach for the line we hurt it".
- B (flashback): the morning after the Sundering; Eryndor holds Malachar's head as Ithren puts him down (matches the film prologue, now from her side).
- C: Torin, as a "security consultant", is introduced to Lyra's project by letter (a phone call, no face yet); he rings Malachar from a station lavatory (the film's scene is a *later* call).
*Cliffhanger:* Seraphine visits the stone where Ithren laid Malachar down, the first time in a century, and finds a paper bird left there within the week. He has been here. He has left her a message she cannot read.

### 4. THE BROKEN KINGDOM
*Logline:* A dig at Ormel Hesh turns into an hour that will not end. (Ormel Hesh is a colony the Aurelith lost on the day of the Sundering; no stone is there.)
- A: Lyra, Hollis and Tuan enter a ruined hill-fort where the afternoon repeats every eleven minutes; a student is nearly lost in the loop.
- B: Auren, hundreds of kilometres away, collapses at work and sees the fort through the loop; he speaks aloud the one instruction that frees them: "Don't hold it. Hand it across." (the two-key principle, learned by accident).
- C: Vesper watches the dig from a ridge and reports to Malachar that "someone has found the lattice".
*Cliffhanger:* Lyra's notebook shows a diagram she did not draw: a ring of seven nodes, with Auren's handwriting of "91717" in the margin.

### 5. THE TRAITOR'S MEMORY
*Logline:* Torin's whole life, and the day he becomes a double agent for good.
- A: Torin as an orphan; Malachar on the end of the bed; the long years; the Wardens (Perrin Hask) who take him in at eighteen without knowing whom he reports to, and expel him at twenty-two.
- B: Hask is dying in a hospice in the mountains; Torin must choose between telling him the truth and stealing the last Warden key. He steals it; Hask forgives him anyway.
- C: Auren sees the scene as a vision (Memory's gift, unbidden) and believes it is Ithren's.
*Cliffhanger:* Hask dies; Torin, alone, says aloud for the first time, "I am the traitor." The Warden key he holds carries the word to the Citadel, and the Lamp tells Seraphine (not Torin: Lock 7).

### 6. THE FIFTH STONE
*Logline:* Malachar goes to take the thing that makes a city build itself forever, and fails.
- A: Malachar and Vesper reach Karth Vael at the edge of the ash. Creation answers to those who want nothing; his desire is the one thing the city cannot ignore. He retreats unharmed; he learns the stone cannot be taken by force.
- B: Anselm Crowe's collection; Vesper steals the Energy stone (brick-red); Corvin carries the weight. This is how Malachar reaches two stones for the film.
- C: Torin joins Lyra's project; Lyra is wary; he gives her the first real clue: Kharesh.
*Cliffhanger:* Malachar, holding two stones in the sanctum, says to the room "Then it will have to be given."

### 7. THE SUNDERING
*Logline:* The full day, told as a confession.
- Structure: Seraphine records a message in the empty shop "for whoever holds the other end" and tells the Sundering at length, intercut with the Aurelith: the argument over the Loom, Verran, the Guardians' secret, the day, Liora, the break, the paper bird. The episode reveals to the audience that the Guardians built the Loom as a throne for themselves, **without** showing that Auren will hear it (the film's Act II confession is a retelling that matters because of what Auren has not been told).
- No contemporary plot beyond the frame. Bottle episode; the season's centre.
*Cliffhanger:* In the recording, Seraphine says "He does not know. He must never know." (She means Malachar; the film pays it off.)

### 8. THE LAST GUARDIAN
*Logline:* The season converges on a pit in a desert.
- A: Seraphine finds in a sealed reliquary at the Citadel a twelve-ringed sigil Ithren hid and a note: "If they wake, the houses must prepare." This is the season's **discovery of the twelve houses**: she learns they exist and are connected to the Loom; she does not know what they are.
- B: Lyra arrives at Kharesh with Hollis and Tuan; Torin is already on site; Auren, by the sea, buys a ticket and does not use it.
- C: Malachar, with two stones, sends Vesper and Corvin to Kharesh; he stays behind and asks the hum "What are you waiting for?"
- Final scene: **the film's first present-day scene**: Lyra on the ramp, Tuan's voice on the radio: "It's a refrigerator in your tent."
*Cliffhanger:* none beyond the film.

## Scripting changes (episodes 101-108)
Where writing a script showed the bible or a beat could not hold with the approved film, the script follows the film and the change is listed here.
- **103:** the stunning of Malachar is staged as the film does, directly after the Sundering, not "the morning after".
- **104:** the object Lyra holds is a lattice token, not a stone; Ormel Hesh has no stone.
- **105:** Torin's timeline corrected (Lock 8); the Lamp speaks only to Seraphine (Lock 7); Torin withholds Auren's name from Malachar, and promises Hask the key will not be used on "the boy", which the film pays off ("you crossed the bridge for me").
- **106:** Creation does not speak to Malachar and Liora does not appear to him at Karth Vael, so the film's courtyard scene is their first reunion; Malachar ends with exactly two stones.
- **107:** the throne, the lie and Liora are staged to match the film's confession; added: the six decide the night before to shear the stones if Verran sits, without Malachar. This is the "He does not know" of the closing line.
- **108:** the twelve-ringed sigil and Ithren's note are the seed of the film's closing line; no sign is named. The episode ends on the film's first scene.

## Season 2 and 3 (high level, reconciled with the film)
- **Season 2, The War of the Guardians (8 episodes).** After the film: the seven stones are in seven lattice cradles, each needing two hands, and the world has no idea who should hold the keys. Malachar's fate is unknown; the twelve signs are lit; the first custodians are chosen badly; Vesper and Torin are on trial; the Reunion splits.
- **Season 3, The Eternal Balance (8 episodes).** The relationship between the seven stones, the nine rings and the twelve houses (the two blank rings are the season's mystery), and the truth behind the First Convergence. This season ties to Zodiac Wars.
- Neither is written beyond this paragraph.
