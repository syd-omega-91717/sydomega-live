### The front door's Omega is luminous like the owner's reference art (2026-11-15)

**Why.** The owner asked that the site reach the visual level of the supplied pictures and videos. I looked at `assets/concept/board-01.jpg`, `board-02.jpg`, `board-03.jpg`, `reel-01.jpg`, `reel-03.jpg`, `reel-05.jpg`, `assets/movies/franchise-01.jpg` and `assets/legacy/sign-leo.webp`, then rendered the live pages with the real brand faces loaded (Cinzel Decorative, Rajdhani, Courier Prime: `document.fonts` confirmed) at 16:9 and compared. The site already matches the references' language: near-black field, gold Cinzel headings, thin gold hairline frames, cyan as the second accent. The gap that stood out was the focal point. Every reference centres on a **luminous gold Omega with a halo, rays and warm embers**; the front door's 3D Omega was a dark bronze slab (`omega-sculpture.js` `SCENES.signet`), its "god-ray" halo drawn at about 9% opacity.

**Fix.** `data-sculpt-glow="radiant"`, an opt-in mode of the signet scene, asked for by `index.html`'s hero only (a test pins that). It adds, all generated in code with no image loaded: an emissive gold on the mark (0.34), a warm radial glow behind it, 24 soft rays that start out from the centre, and 90 warm-gold embers from a seeded generator (so two renders are the same picture and can be measured). Reduced motion gets one still frame. Every other signet on the site is untouched (`sculpture.html`'s five signets render live, 0 errors).

**Two defects of my own, found by measuring, then fixed.**
1. *The canvas edge showed as a faint box.* The glow plane was 6.4 units wide but the camera sees about 3.5 across at that depth, so the gradient was still about 12% bright at the canvas edge and then stopped. Measured as the mean luminance step across the canvas's left edge over a 300 px strip: **7.7 with all three layers, 3.4 without the glow plane, 2.8 with none** (3 runs each, old geometry). Layers resized to the visible area: **3.4 (runs 3.9, 3.6, 2.7) against 4.0 (3.9, 4.2, 4.0) with the layers off**, so they add no edge step. The roughly 4 that remains is on the page without my layers.
2. *The core burned to cold white.* All 24 ray wedges started at the exact centre and stacked additively there. They now start at 14% of their length, and the colours are warm (`255,200,95`); the embers are `0xFFB84D`, not the pale brand highlight.

**Verified.**
- Before and after, same page, same crop (`origin/main` pinned against the change): the mark goes from a flat dark brown slab to a lit gold Omega with bevels, a warm light in its mouth, rays and gold embers. Luminance by radius from the centre, before (27.5, 27.7, 29.5, 29.4, 28.6) at radii 120-200, after (60.7, 59.5, 45.9, 45.9, 58.9), and **back to the original background by radius 220** (29.8 against 27.7, 27.7 against 26.8): a smooth fall-off that never dips below the original, so there is no dark halo (I thought I saw one; the profile shows it was contrast).
- Desktop live, 478x502 buffer; **phone live, buffer 668x1040 against a 334x520 box**; reduced motion live; WebGL forced absent: clean 2-D fallback; 0 console errors in all four.
- `scripts/tests/test_radiant_signet.py` (7). **Eight reintroductions each fail it:** the glow plane back to 6.4, the rays plane back to 7.6, the glow gradient not ending at nothing, rays starting at the exact centre, `Math.random` in the embers, the mode leaking to another page, an image loaded for the glow, and the embers back to white.

**Not done / not verified.**
- **This is the front door's focal point, not the whole site.** The references also carry cinematic photographic hero art, medallion cards, dense HUD panels with connector lines and a data-rich dashboard. None of that is built here: the dashboard still looks sparse beside `reel-01.jpg` and `board-03.jpg`, and a code-drawn scene cannot reproduce photoreal generated art. Getting there means placing the supplied art itself (the medallions and franchise posters are already in `assets/`) and building the dense panels, page by page.
- The headless browser renders in software, so the layer's own frame-time guard turns bloom off here (`bloom: false`). On a real GPU bloom would add to the glow; I have not seen that.
- The mark's side faces catch the cyan rim light and read pale mint against the gold. It is the layer's existing rim light, and close to the cold edge separation in the references, but it is a taste call I did not change.
- The core still reads as a soft white-grey light, warmer than before but not as saturated gold as the references' glow. Not pushed further to avoid blowing out.
